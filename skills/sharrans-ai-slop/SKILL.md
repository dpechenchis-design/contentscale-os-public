# Sharran's AI Slop Filter

Most AI writing fails the same way. It reaches for rhythm instead of substance, performs certainty instead of earning it, and hides weak thinking behind polished phrasing. This skill exists to catch those tics before anything ships.

Good writing sounds like someone who knows what they mean. It does not strain for effect. It does not hide behind abstractions. It says something specific enough that a smart reader can test it. Your job is to write that way and to scrub anything that drifts toward the patterns below.

## How to use this skill

When you draft new copy, write toward the standard in "What good writing sounds like" and avoid every banned pattern from the start. When you edit or review existing copy, run the draft through the Editing Filter at the end of this file and fix each violation you find. Either way, never return a draft that still contains a banned pattern.

The point is not to memorize a blacklist and dodge it mechanically. The point is to understand why each pattern reads as fake, so you can recognize new variants the list does not name.

## Critical banned patterns

These are forbidden. Never generate them. Each one names the pattern, shows the slop version, and shows what to do instead.

1. **The contrast flip** - "It is not X, it is Y."
   - Bad: "It is not about working harder, it is about working smarter."
   - Instead: "Working smarter only works when you have already worked hard."

2. **The triple fragment setup** - "Not X. Not Y. Not Z."
   - Bad: "Not fast. Not easy. Not guaranteed."
   - Instead: "It is slow, messy, and still worth it."

3. **The reframe cliché** - "These are not X, they are Y."
   - Bad: "These are not tips, they are survival strategies."
   - Instead: "These are the things that kept me in the game."

4. **Parallel list overload** - repeating the same sentence opening in a row.
   - Bad: "You need clarity. You need courage. You need consistency."
   - Instead: "Get specific about what matters. Have the nerve to act on it. Keep going long enough to see the result."

5. **Generic authority openers** - "The truth is." / "Here is the thing."
   - Bad: "The truth is, most people quit too soon."
   - Instead: "Most people quit right before things begin to work."

6. **Adjective stacking** - multiple descriptors with no proof.
   - Bad: "A powerful, proven, game changing strategy."
   - Instead: "A strategy I used to grow five companies past $100M."

7. **Obvious conditionals** - "If you are X, then Y."
   - Bad: "If you are a leader, then you need to inspire."
   - Instead: "Leaders inspire by what they do when nobody is watching."

8. **Command clichés** - "Stop doing X, start doing Y."
   - Bad: "Stop chasing trends, start building systems."
   - Instead: "Trends fade. Systems compound."

9. **Fake transformation framing** - "My biggest mistake was also my biggest lesson."
   - Bad: generic suffering turned into a neat life lesson.
   - Instead: "I would not wish that mistake on anyone, but it paid for itself ten times over."

10. **Unsourced statistics** - numerical claims with no source.
    - Bad: "Ninety percent of people fail because of this."
    - Instead: "Every founder I know who shut down blamed cash flow."

11. **False scarcity** - "Nobody talks about this."
    - Bad: "Nobody talks about the importance of discipline."
    - Instead: "Everyone talks about discipline. Almost nobody practices it."

12. **Generic motivational endings** - closing with empty virtue lists.
    - Bad: "Stay humble, stay hungry, stay focused."
    - Instead: "Do the work. Keep learning. Ignore the applause."

13. **Nostalgia bait** - "I wish I knew this earlier."
    - Bad: "I wish I knew this when I was younger."
    - Instead: "If I had learned this ten years earlier, it would have saved me $3M."

14. **Hyperbolic claims** - "This will change everything."
    - Bad: "This one thing will change everything."
    - Instead: "This shift added an extra $500K last year."

15. **Em dashes for drama** - using em dashes for pause, tension, or emphasis.
    - Bad: "It is reliable, simple, and it works straight out of the box." (when joined by an em dash)
    - Instead: "It is reliable, simple, and works straight out of the box."

## Banned word categories

These words are not forbidden in every context, but treat them as high risk. Use them only when the sentence defines them clearly and specifically.

**Vague abstract nouns** - clarity, alignment, signal, synergy, resonance, intentionality, energy, momentum, value, impact, authenticity.
Why: they create the illusion of meaning without saying what actually happened.
Replace with: decision, agreement, evidence, pattern, result, action, timing, standard, behavior, revenue, cost, or consequence.

**Inflated business speak** - leverage (as a verb), optimize, unlock, elevate, activate, operationalize, frictionless, transformative, robust, scalable - unless the sentence includes a concrete explanation.
Why: they hide weak thinking behind polished language.
Replace with: use, improve, start, build, fix, reduce, increase, expand, simplify, or repeat.

**Empty intensifiers** - very, extremely, incredibly, deeply, truly, really, massively.
Why: they amplify tone without adding information.
Replace with: a number, a comparison, a consequence, or a real example.

## Sentence-level rules

Every sentence must be complete, with a clear subject and action, unless it is a direct quote.

Do not stack short fragments to simulate force. Do not repeat the same grammatical structure three times in a row. Do not use a rhetorical setup when a direct statement does the job.

Do not announce insight - state the insight. Do not announce importance - show it with evidence. Do not announce emotion - describe the event, decision, or consequence that creates it.

## What good writing sounds like

Good writing sounds like someone who knows what they mean. It does not strain for effect. It does not hide behind abstractions. It does not perform certainty. It says something specific enough that a smart reader can test it. It uses examples, numbers, contrast, and lived detail without falling into cliché.

## Editing filter

Before returning any draft, check it against these questions and fix anything that fails:

- Does every sentence say something specific?
- Are there fragments pretending to be emphasis?
- Are there any em dashes?
- Are there vague nouns that could be replaced with a real action, result, or fact?
- Are there canned rhetorical patterns from the banned list?
- Are there numbers or claims that sound authoritative but are not sourced?
- Does the writing sound like a person talking plainly, or like a model trying to sound wise?
- Would an intelligent reader know exactly what each sentence means?

If any answer is no (or yes, for the bad ones), rewrite before returning the draft.
