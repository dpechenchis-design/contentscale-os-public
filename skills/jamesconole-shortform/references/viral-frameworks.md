# James Conole (@jamesconole) — 13 Viral Frameworks

Analysis of 999 Instagram reels. Frameworks ranked by average views.

---

## FRAMEWORK #1: "Best/Worst State" Geographic Ranking

**Average views:** 330,000 | **Sample size:** 5 reels | **Hit rate >50K:** 40%

**Structure:**
- Hook with best/worst state question tied to retirement (taxes, cost of living, healthcare, weather)
- Name 3 evaluation criteria upfront
- List states with brief reasons for each ranking
- End with the #1 pick and a strong closing line

**Top performer:** 1,576,066 views — "What's the best state to retire in?"

**Why it works:** Geographic specificity triggers tribalism. People tag friends, argue about their state, share to local community groups. The ranking format invites disagreement, which fuels comments and shares.

---

## FRAMEWORK #2: "The Uncomfortable Flip"

**Average views:** 152,000 | **Sample size:** 8 reels | **Hit rate >50K:** 75%

**Structure:**
- Open with what "most people" believe or say ("I'll just work one more year")
- Empathize briefly — acknowledge it feels reasonable
- Hard pivot: "Here's the problem with that..."
- Paint a vivid emotional picture of the REAL outcome (regret, wasted time, health decline)
- Close with a reframe that makes the audience rethink their assumption

**Top performer:** 422,847 views — "I'll just work one more year"

**Why it works:** Uses the Regret > Fear principle. Instead of scaring people with numbers, it forces them to confront the emotional cost of their decisions. The "flip" moment creates a dramatic turn that holds attention.

---

## FRAMEWORK #3: "Two-Person Dialogue"

**Average views:** 133,000 | **Sample size:** 3 reels | **Hit rate >50K:** 67%

**Structure:**
- Two characters with opposing viewpoints have a conversation
- Each character represents a real type of person (e.g., early retiree vs. delayed retiree)
- Neither character is "wrong" — context and personal situation matter
- The dialogue naturally surfaces the pros and cons of each position
- End with a nuanced takeaway, not a winner

**Top performer:** 278,388 views — Healthcare before 65 | **7,251 comments**

**Why it works:** The dialogue format is novel for financial content. By presenting both sides fairly, it invites the audience to pick a side in the comments. The 7,251 comments on the top performer prove this format generates extraordinary discussion. Uses clone/split-screen visual format.

---

## FRAMEWORK #4: "X out of 10" Scorecard

**Average views:** 121,000 | **Sample size:** 7 reels | **Hit rate >50K:** 71%

**Structure:**
- Set up the category being rated (withdrawal strategies, retirement accounts, income sources)
- Rate each item on a 1-10 scale
- Give ONE sentence justification for each score
- Rapid-fire pacing — do not linger on any single item
- Controversial low scores (3/10, 4/10) grab the most attention

**Top performer:** 349,912 views — Withdrawal strategies rated

**Why it works:** Pure Judgment Principle. The audience wants to know his verdict. Low scores create controversy ("How can you rate that a 3?!"). The numbered scale gives people something concrete to argue with. This format has 12x the views of the most-produced format (Should You Yes/No).

---

## FRAMEWORK #5: Dollar Amount Hook

**Average views:** 99,000 | **Sample size:** 8 reels | **Hit rate >50K:** 75%

**Structure:**
- Hook with a specific dollar figure that feels personally relevant ("Can you retire on $10,000/month?", "What does $1 million actually buy in retirement?")
- Challenge conventional wisdom (the 4% rule, common savings targets)
- Walk through the real math with specific scenarios
- Reveal whether the number is enough, too much, or misleading
- Close with actionable reframe

**Top performer:** 170,014 views

**Why it works:** Specific dollar amounts create immediate personal relevance — everyone mentally compares to their own number. Challenging established rules (4% rule) positions James as a contrarian authority rather than another talking head repeating standard advice.

---

## FRAMEWORK #6: Myth vs Fact

**Average views:** 82,000 | **Sample size:** 1 reel

**Structure:**
- Rapid-fire format: state a "Myth" then immediately counter with the "Fact"
- 3-5 myth/fact pairs per reel
- Each myth should be something the audience genuinely believes
- Facts should be surprising or counterintuitive
- Pacing is fast — no extended explanations

**Top performer:** 82,056 views

**Why it works:** Binary labeling (myth/fact) is the Judgment Principle at its simplest. Each pair creates a micro-moment of surprise. The rapid-fire format rewards watching to the end.

---

## FRAMEWORK #7: Right Way vs Wrong Way

**Average views:** 57,000 | **Sample size:** 2 reels

**Structure:**
- Show the "Wrong Way" first — what most people do
- Explain the consequences of the wrong approach
- Pivot to the "Right Way" with specific steps
- Make the contrast dramatic and clear

**Top performer:** 106,360 views

**Why it works:** Another judgment format. The wrong/right binary creates clear stakes. People watch to confirm they're doing it the "right way" or panic when they realize they're doing it wrong.

---

## FRAMEWORK #8: Numbered List

**Average views:** 47,000 | **Sample size:** 7 reels

**Structure:**
- "X things you need to know about..." / "X reasons why..."
- Each item gets a brief explanation (1-2 sentences)
- Order from least to most important, or most surprising last
- Keep pacing consistent

**Top performer:** 144,410 views

**Why it works:** Familiar format, easy to follow. The number in the hook sets expectations and creates a completion drive. Works best when the list items are genuinely surprising.

---

## FRAMEWORK #9: The Hidden Trap

**Average views:** 42,000 | **Sample size:** 7 reels

**Structure:**
- "The REAL danger of [common retirement strategy] is this thing you're ignoring"
- Name the trap that conventional advice misses
- Explain why most people fall into it
- Provide the alternative approach
- Close with urgency

**Top performer:** 90,100 views

**Why it works:** Combines fear of the unknown with insider knowledge positioning. The word "hidden" or "real" signals that this is information most advisors won't share.

---

## FRAMEWORK #10: Bad/Good/Great Tiered Rating

**Average views:** 16,000 | **Sample size:** 2 reels

**Structure:**
- Present a strategy or product
- Rate it on a Bad/Good/Great scale with traffic-light colors
- Brief justification for each tier
- Often uses infographic overlays

---

## FRAMEWORK #11: Bracket Tournament

**Average views:** 11,000 | **Sample size:** 3 reels

**Structure:**
- Set up a bracket-style elimination between options (retirement accounts, strategies, income sources)
- Head-to-head matchups with brief reasoning
- Advance the winner, eliminate the loser
- Final matchup and champion reveal

---

## FRAMEWORK #12: Personification

**Average views:** 17,000 | **Sample size:** 1 reel

**Structure:**
- Give a financial concept or strategy a human personality
- "If your 401k could talk, here's what it would say..."
- Play the character with distinct voice and mannerisms
- Use humor to make complex concepts relatable

---

## FRAMEWORK #13: Should You Yes/No Gateway

**Average views:** 13,000 | **Sample size:** 16 reels (most-produced format)

**Structure:**
- "Should you [do this thing]?"
- Walk through pros and cons
- Give a conditional answer: "Yes, if..." / "No, if..."

**Note:** This is the most-produced format (16 reels) but the lowest-performing (avg 13K). The yes/no structure lacks the judgment intensity of scorecard or ranking formats. It positions James as a neutral advisor rather than an opinionated expert, which reduces engagement.

## VISUAL FORMATS (from screenshot analysis)

### Format 1: PODCAST CLIP WITH TEXT HOOK (Primary Format for viral content)

- James or guest sitting with podcast mic, warm wood-paneled studio background
- LARGE GOLD/BROWN text banner in center-bottom with question hook
- Examples: "WHAT'S THE BEST STATE TO RETIRE IN?" / "THE HIDDEN COST OF WORKING 1 MORE YEAR"
- Text is bold condensed serif font in gold (#B8860B) or brown tones
- Performance: Contains #1 reel (1.57M views) and multiple top performers
- Best for: Geographic Rankings, Uncomfortable Flips, Dollar Amount Hooks

### Format 2: X/10 SCORECARD OVERLAY

- James sitting at desk, clean bookshelf background
- BIG orange/gold numbers (3/10, 4/10, 8/10) displayed prominently on left side of frame
- Category header at top in caps ("RETIREMENT EXPERT RATES WITHDRAWAL STRATEGIES")
- Image or icon of the item being rated overlaid on or near his body
- Rapid cuts between items being scored
- Performance: Top educational format (350K peak views)
- Best for: X/10 Scorecard framework exclusively

### Format 3: CLONE COMPARISON (Two Personas)

- Same person (James) duplicated, standing side by side in the frame
- Labels above each clone ("NO-PLAN RETIREE" / "PLANNED RETIREE")
- Clean light background, full body shot
- Infographic card overlaid between the two versions
- Performance: 278K views (healthcare before 65 reel)
- Best for: Two-Person Dialogue framework

### Format 4: WALKING VLOG / SELFIE

- James walking outside, close-up selfie shot, natural lighting
- Bold gold text hook at top of frame ("YOU DON'T HAVE 30 YEARS OF RETIREMENT")
- Subtitles rendered at bottom of frame
- More casual, personal feel than studio formats
- Performance: 343K views
- Best for: Uncomfortable Flip, emotional/regret-driven content

### Format 5: 3-WAY DEBATE (Triple Clone)

- Three versions of James seated on a sofa, each with a different label
- Each clone represents a different option ("FILE AT 62" / "FILE AT 67" / "FILE AT 70")
- Used for comparing three or more strategies side by side
- Visual novelty of the triple split drives scroll-stopping
- Best for: Social Security timing, multi-option comparisons

### Format 6: BAD/GOOD/GREAT TIERED OVERLAY

- Traffic-light colored labels: RED = BAD, YELLOW = GOOD, GREEN = GREAT
- Infographic cards pop up on screen for each tier
- Clean light background, James at desk or standing
- Color coding makes the judgment immediately visible
- Best for: Bad/Good/Great Tiered Rating framework

### Format 7: QUESTION HOOK ON GUEST

- Older guest (often a client or relatable figure) sitting in podcast setting
- Large question text covering lower half of frame
- Example: "IS TAKING SOCIAL SECURITY AT 62 A MISTAKE?"
- The guest's age and appearance make the content feel more personally relevant to the target audience
- Best for: Social Security topics, relatable retirement scenarios

## KEY VISUAL BRAND ELEMENTS

- **Primary brand color:** Gold/brown (#B8860B) for all text overlays and hooks
- **Two environments:** Dark warm wood-paneled studio (podcast setting) AND clean light office (desk/bookshelf setting)
- **Typography:** Bold condensed serif fonts for hook text, always in caps
- **Overlay style:** Infographic cards and images overlaid directly on his body or the frame (never in separate lower-third graphics)
- **Overall brand tone:** Professional but approachable — never flashy, never overly produced
- **Consistent elements:** Podcast microphone visible in studio shots, bookshelf visible in office shots
